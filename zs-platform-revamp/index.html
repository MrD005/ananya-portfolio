Context & challenge

The opportunity

Two tools shape one connected internal journey.

Creating a project and assigning the right people are closely linked, but users experience them through two different tools.

The revamp began with a broad question: where does the current experience create friction, and which improvements matter most to users and the product?

My role is to build shared understanding before redesigning—learning both platforms, documenting goals and gaps, gathering user evidence, and keeping product and engineering aligned around the same plan.

Design question

How might we make project creation and allocation easier to understand, complete and manage across two connected tools?

03

Research process

Learn before redesigning

Combine product immersion with direct user evidence.

  1. 01

    Build platform understanding

    Complete product training and use both tools firsthand to learn terminology, roles, dependencies and the end-to-end experience.

  2. 02

    Document the current state

    Maintain separate UX reports, define business, user and product goals, map major usability gaps, and agree on timelines and actions.

  3. 03

    Plan consistent interviews

    Create a discussion guide around real tasks, expectations, workarounds and pain points so conversations generate comparable evidence.

  4. 04

    Synthesize and connect insights

    Turn interview observations into themes and map each insight back to the relevant goal and gap in the UX tracker.

  5. 05

    Prioritize the design response

    Align the evidence with product and engineering constraints before improving the workflows and interface.

Interview guide

Move from opinions to task-based evidence.

The guide keeps interviews focused while leaving room to uncover unexpected behavior and context.

  1. 01

    ContextRole, responsibilities and frequency of use

  2. 02

    Recent taskWhat the user tried to achieve and why

  3. 03

    WalkthroughSteps, decisions, handoffs and workarounds

  4. 04

    ReflectionFriction, confidence and desired improvement

05

Validation & next steps

Closing the loop

Validate with users, learn from UAT and iterate.

The project is ongoing. So far, 7 user interviews surfaced 32 usability gaps, and 24 recommendations have been accepted into the product roadmap. Product outcomes are still evidence to earn through UAT, not numbers to predict.

After product and engineering alignment, the next cycle is to design against prioritized gaps, run UAT with representative users, map feedback back into the tracker, and refine the experience before release.

Success will be measured on three metrics: user satisfaction, task efficiency and adoption of the revamped tools.
